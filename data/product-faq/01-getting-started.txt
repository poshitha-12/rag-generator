Tidewater Sensors — Product FAQ
Getting Started

What is the Tidewater TS-40? The TS-40 is a fictional battery-powered water
quality sensor used to monitor rivers, reservoirs and aquaculture ponds. It
measures temperature, dissolved oxygen, turbidity and pH, and reports over
cellular or LoRaWAN. This document is sample data for demonstrating the RAG
Generator and does not describe a real product.

How do I set up a new sensor? Unpack the sensor, remove the protective cap
from the probe head, and press the pairing button for three seconds until
the status light blinks amber. Open the Tidewater app, choose Add Device,
and scan the QR code on the sensor housing. Setup usually takes under five
minutes.

How long does the battery last? A TS-40 running the default fifteen-minute
reporting interval lasts about eighteen months on one battery pack. At a
five-minute interval, expect roughly seven months. Cold water shortens
battery life; below 4 degrees Celsius, plan for about 20 percent less.

How deep can the sensor be deployed? The TS-40 is rated to 30 metres. The
extended-cable variant, the TS-40L, reaches 100 metres but requires the
powered surface buoy because the longer cable draws more current.

What connectivity does it need? Either a cellular SIM, which ships
pre-installed on cellular units, or a LoRaWAN gateway within about two
kilometres in open terrain. Sensors buffer up to 30 days of readings
locally, so a temporary loss of connectivity does not lose data.
